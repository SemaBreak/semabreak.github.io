I remember the first time I got introduced to LLMs (pre-agent explosion). It was during a short stint at a tiny social startup. My mentor / boss was seriously into AI and true believer. After many a task where I refused to use AI, I gave it a shot and was blown away.
 
It wasn't all knowing, but it helped me locate all the stupid simple mistakes I made in a code block. I still had to do the legwork to narrow dowm the buggy code, so it felt like a good comprimise between thinking/doing and outsourcing.

I continued with this method of using AI for a while, until in my first corporate role, one of my collegue informed me of the agent boom and was shocked to see me still manually copying code to a LLM. I was working with VS Code at the time, and when agent mode kicked off, it was the second time AI blew me away.

It was able to save me the legwork I previously had to do and while initially amazing, I felt it would get out of hand quickly and I would just "vibe-code" it all (a term I REALLY DISLIKED at the time) and lose all competancy.

So I had to make a choice - how would I treat the now legitimaly powerful AI now? I wasn't afraid of it taking my job, I was afraid of me just becoming a monkey hitting a typewriter, not understanding what I was doing.

I decided on a two pronged strategy / plan - in my corporate role, I was expected to be as efficent and hyped as I can be with AI, so I let agents run most of the show coding wise, but left the architecture to me, while insisting on understadning the root cause of most errors. And for every personal / non-urgent project, I will use AI mostly as a teacher in a socratic-like dialouge. This approach served me well so far.

But I am not blind to the downsides of my approah, the main one being - I am slowly becoming less "coding" capabale, while still growing as an engineer (which is why I heavily disagree with the idea that "coding was never the hard part").

All I know is it is a brave new world out there, and I just hope I can learn to adjust correctly to the ever shifting tides (which have become massive) in softwrae engineering.
